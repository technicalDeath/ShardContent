# New mage playtest: what a new player runs into

**2026-10-14.** The assistant played a brand-new Pure Mage (template 11, still Young, no staff help except where noted) for about three hours with a bot that grows a decision tree each time it meets something it cannot handle: `tests/scenarios/new-mage-playtest/mage_bot.py` (run it with `run_bot.ps1`; raw findings go to `work/mage-playtest/notes.jsonl`, the run log to `run.log`). Nothing here has been built or approved; it is a to-do list for the owner to rank.

Character at the start: 74 hit points, 39 mana, Magery 50, Evaluating Intelligence 35, Resisting Spells 35, 500 gold, carrying 130 of 208 stones. Host: a disposable copy of the deployed build, Britain, Felucca.

## What the bot does now (the tree)

Each tick the first rule that acts wins: `r_dead` (walk to the healer as a ghost and accept the offer) > `r_flee` (low health: heal, drink, run) > `r_avoid` (a monster that is not safe prey: fight it from range if there is mana, else step away) > `r_heal` > `r_fight` (magic arrow at safe prey, keep distance) > `r_loot` > `r_resupply` (out of reagents: walk to the mage shop, open the vendor's list, buy) > `r_train` (ask the guild trainers what they teach) > `r_hunt` (walk up to prey) > `r_rest` (heal, Meditate) > `r_explore` (a ring of waypoints, skipping places that have killed it, remembered in `knowledge.json`). Each rule is there because a run got stuck or died without it: the file's header lists them in the order they were added.

## The smarter bot against the first one (same template, same town)

The first bot was a rule list with a hand-written animal list and one attack spell. The second reads the server's numbers (`game_data.json`, built by `make_game_data.py` from the source) through `knowledge.py`: it works out for each monster how many casts the kill takes and how much it hits for meanwhile, picks the attack spell by damage per second and per gold at the mage's current skill, casts the highest circle that can still teach when the target is easy, and measures the damage and skill gains it actually sees.

| | First bot (about 3 hours) | Second bot (13 minutes) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deaths | 13 | 0 |
| Spells cast | Magic Arrow and Heal only | 6 Fireball, 23 Lightning, plus buffs when a fight was hard |
| Magery | 50.0 base, unchanged | 52.9 to 54.4 (6 gains in 29 casts) |
| Evaluating Intelligence | 35 to 42 over the run | 35 to 37.2 in 13 minutes |
| Gold | 500 down to 9 (a counting mistake bought reagents it did not need) | 500, untouched (the starter bag holds 50 of each reagent) |
| Time resting | most of it | most of it: mana, not health, is the limit (Meditation went 5 to 14) |

The measured damage is larger than the formula's: Lightning shows about 27 a cast against the formula's 12 to 20 (Evaluating Intelligence scales it), so the bot's estimates start from the code's numbers and then use what the game shows.
## The list

Priority: **A** a new player is likely to die, quit or get stuck without it; **B** wastes a lot of time or confuses; **C** polish. "Evidence" says how it was seen.

### Survival

1. **A. The west road kills a level-one mage and nothing warns about it.** Within about 150 tiles of the Britain gate toward the north-west there are orcs, ratmen, trolls, harpies, bears and a panther; the bot died there more than ten times (positions in the run's death notes, around 1300,1485 and 1330,1559) before it learned the places and stopped walking to them. Idea: a "Where to hunt" page in the Welcome guide's Your first hour (rats, birds, rabbits and hinds just east and south of the town are safe; the north-west wilds are not), beginner hunting grounds as markers in `UORekindled-Britain.csv`, and an overhead warning (or a one-time tip) the first time a player is within a few tiles of a monster that is far stronger than they are. Effort: a day for the text and markers; the warning is a client feature.
2. **A. Monsters follow a new player into Britain and nothing in town stops them.** An orc-like ratman ("Chickeki"), a troll, a harpy and a brown bear each chased the mage inside the walls, once into the square beside the healer (1463,1612). No guard was within 14 tiles of the healer in any check. This also threatens a ghost who walks to the healer. Idea: guards attack monsters that come inside the town (stock guard behavior needs checking), or monsters stop at the gate. Evidence: bot log "cornered by ..." lines at 1469,1619. Needs a design ruling (it changes how Britain plays).
3. **A. A resurrected mage comes back at 10 hit points and about no mana, in the street.** It then rests for about four minutes; every Heal costs mana it does not have. Idea: say what to do on the way back ("Rest or use Meditation; the healer's street is safe"), and consider coming back with more health. Evidence: the log's `resting: mana 1/40, hp 10/74` and the 3 to 4 minute recovery.
4. **B. A ghost is told how to get back, but not where the healer is.** The bot only walked to the right place because it was given the coordinates. Idea: the ghost strip (which already shows the way to the body) also names the nearest healer and points to it; the Britain map already marks "Healer (resurrects you)" at 1471,1611. Evidence: `r_dead`; the offer then opens by itself as a Resurrection gump (CONTINUE or CANCEL) when the ghost is within a few tiles of the healer, which worked. Later farm runs found the catch: a ghost is offered only if it is in war mode and walks into the healer's range, and nothing says so (see the ghost item in `To-Do-List.md`).
5. **B. Low-health options for a mage with no mana are unclear.** At 20 to 30 percent health with 0 mana the bot could only run. The starter potions are shown as colors in the headless client; in the real client the potion bar and tooltips name them, so this may be fine for a human, but the bot could not tell which potion heals and lost hit points trying (+3, 0, -7, -3, -10 in five drinks, partly monster hits). Check that the potion bar and the first-time tip make "Heal potion at low health" obvious for a mage (the `autoUse` Heal potion switch helps if it is on).

### Reagents, mana and money

6. **A. When the reagents run out, casting stops with only "More reagents are needed for this spell."** The starter bag holds 50 of each of the eight reagents (found by opening it: the first run only counted the three loose stacks in the pack and wrongly concluded 30, and bought 408 gold of reagents it did not need). That is about 50 casts of any one spell, roughly a quarter hour of hunting with Magic Arrow; then the message appears with no word on which reagent is missing or where to buy it. Idea: a one-time tip when any reagent is down to 5 ("Reagents are sold at the Mage shop in Britain, about 3 gold each: 1485,1550"), and make the message name the missing reagent. Also tell a new mage that the bag exists and what is in it: nothing in the pack says "reagents inside".
7. **B. The Mage shop works, but the way in is hidden.** In the headless client the shop opened by saying "<vendor name> buy" next to the shopkeeper (Lauryn); prices: Sulfurous Ash, Spiders' Silk, Nightshade, Mandrake, Ginseng, Garlic 3 gold each, Blood Moss and Black Pearl 5, empty bottle 5, potions 15 to 21 gold. Idea: a "Shopping" line in the guide ("double-click a shopkeeper or say its name and buy"); check what the real client does on a double-click.
8. **B. Mana runs out after about nine Magic Arrows and the way to get it back is not mentioned.** Meditation is there (14 at the start, it trained to 29 in half an hour) and brings mana back much faster than waiting, but nothing tells a new mage. Its failure message, "You cannot focus your concentration.", is also unexplained: it appeared when something hostile was near or the mage had just been hurt. Idea: a first-time caster tip when mana first drops below a quarter ("Meditation: use it while nobody is around to get mana back faster"), and say why it fails ("...with an enemy so close").
9. **C. "You must wait a few moments to use another skill."** Meditation repeated within about ten seconds gives this; harmless, but a player spamming the skill sees it constantly. Check the wording against the era's.
10. **B. The spell bar opens on Circle 6.** In the real client a brand-new mage with Magery 50 gets a bar of eight 20-mana, high-circle spells (screenshot `work/player-client/MageP/shots/20261014-162401-first-screen.png`); `SpellBarGump.HighestCastable` picks the highest circle the skill allows, but a new mage has few mana, few reagents and, as far as the bot could see, a book of early spells. A new player will click these and watch them fail. Idea: open on the first circle with spells the character can actually cast (mana and reagents), or on Circle 1; mark spells that are not in the book.

### Time wasters and noise

11. **B. Every vermin kill opens an empty corpse window.** Rats, birds and the like drop nothing; each corpse still opens by itself (the auto-open default), and the bot logged `corpse ...: []` for almost every kill. The client has a "skip empty corpses" switch that is off. Idea: turn it on in `default-profile.json` (the key is `skip_empty_corpse`, confirmed in a real `profile.json`; the defaults doc's test pins such names).
12. **C. Weight.** The mage starts at 130 to 160 stones of 208 (about three quarters): eleven potions, three scrolls, a bedroll, kindling, a candle, scissors, a recall rune, bags. Not a problem while walking, but a hunt that picks anything up fills the pack. Idea: look at what a mage really needs on the first day (the `StarterWeightBudget` doc already has the budget).
13. **C. The trainers offer nothing for the mage's own skills.** At the Mage shop Belora offered Inscription and Wrestling, Lauryn Alchemy and Taste Identification, Merill Inscription (on its first try a trainer sometimes answered only after a few seconds, so ask again before deciding nobody answered). Magery, Evaluating Intelligence, Meditation and Resisting Spells were not offered (the shard's rule is that a trainer teaches while you know less than a third of what he knows, so the mage is past that for Magery and Resisting; Meditation, at 14 to 29, was not offered either). Idea: check whether Meditation should be trainable at that level, and have a trainer say "I have nothing to teach you" when the list is empty.
14. **B. Once the starting gold is spent a mage has no way to earn more.** No animal the mage killed in the runs dropped any gold (vermin corpses were empty every time; the monsters that carry gold are the ones it cannot beat yet, see item 16) and the shop's sell list for the starting pack named only reagents at 2 to 3 gold each: the potions, scrolls, candle, bedroll and scissors are not bought. With the 400 starter reagents this is not urgent on the first day (the first run spent its gold only because of a counting mistake), but a mage that keeps hunting has no income. Idea: tell the player what a day's budget is, let ordinary animals drop a few coins or hides, or add a beginner way to earn. Needs a design ruling, since it touches the economy.
15. **A. The player is never told which actions can raise a skill and which cannot.** This is the finding that mattered most. The server's rule (`SkillCheck.CheckLocation`, the numbers in `tests/scenarios/new-mage-playtest/game_data.json`): a use whose difficulty window the skill has passed always succeeds and **rolls no gain at all**. For Magery the window of a spell is 0 to 40 for circle 1, 10 to 50 for circle 2, 20 to 60 for circle 3, 30 to 70 for circle 4, 40 to 80 for circle 5, and so on up by ten. A new mage starts at 50, so **Magic Arrow, Heal, Reactive Armor and every other first- and second-circle cast can never raise Magery again**, however many it casts: the first run cast about 150 of them in half an hour and Magery stayed at a base of 50.0. The second run, which cast Fireball (circle 3) and Lightning (circle 4) at animals, gained Magery within a dozen casts (the log line `GAIN Magery +0.1 -> 53.4 (from lightning)`), and Evaluating Intelligence rose from the same casts. Meditation, by contrast, gained 0.1 to 0.4 on most uses from a low start. Nothing in the game says any of this: the player sees mana and reagents disappear with no gain and no explanation. Ideas, smallest first: (a) the Skill Guide page for each skill states what trains it and the window ("Magery: cast a spell of the circle just above what you find easy; first and second circle stop teaching at 50"); (b) a one-time message the first time an action stops being able to teach ("You have learned all you can from casting Magic Arrow"), repeated per action at most once; (c) in the Skills window, a tooltip on a skill that lists the actions that can still teach it at its current value; (d) the same fact in the first-hour page of the guide for each starting template. The table of windows for every skill is data the server already has; for the other skills it needs a pass of the same kind (mining, blacksmithy, archery, etc. each have their own min and max per action).
The Magery windows (from the server's own table; the skill value the game shows includes stat bonuses, so a mage at 50 base shows about 53):

| Circle | Window (skill) | At Magery 50 | At Magery 70 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0 to 40 | no gain (always succeeds) | no gain (always succeeds) |
| 2 | 10 to 50 | no gain (always succeeds) | no gain (always succeeds) |
| 3 | 20 to 60 | teaches, succeeds 75% | no gain (always succeeds) |
| 4 | 30 to 70 | teaches, succeeds 50% | no gain (always succeeds) |
| 5 | 40 to 80 | teaches, succeeds 25% | teaches, succeeds 75% |
| 6 | 50 to 90 | teaches, succeeds 0% | teaches, succeeds 50% |
| 7 | 60 to 100 | fizzles (too hard) | teaches, succeeds 25% |
| 8 | 70 to 110 | fizzles (too hard) | teaches, succeeds 0% |

Two more rules of the same kind, found in the same code, that a player has no way to know: below 10.0 a skill gains on every use (which is why Meditation went from about 5 to 12 in ten minutes of resting), and above it each gain is a roll that is slower the nearer the skill is to the top of the window. The shard's gain-curve classes (`/SkillClasses`) change the rolls but not the no-challenge rule.
16. **C. Looting and banking are learned by trial.** Taking from a corpse needs the player to stand next to it: from two tiles the server answers "That is out of sight.", and a pick-up straight after opening is refused with "You must wait to perform another action." until the action delay passes. A corpse of a monster someone else killed also says "Looting this monster corpse will be a criminal act!" (the shard's Loot Protection). The monsters that carry gold are the ones a new mage cannot beat yet: corpses of a troll, lizardman, orc and mongbat (killed by staff for the loot test) held 105 to 118, 53 to 92, 46 to 58 and 7 to 12 gold, plus equipment and parts nobody needs; vermin held nothing. The bank box opens only when the player says "bank" next to a banker; gold goes in by dragging it onto the box and comes out by saying "withdraw <amount>" ("balance" tells the sum). The bot, which now loots (gold, reagents, potions, scrolls and gems; it leaves equipment and parts) and banks (junk and gold above a 200 reserve when heavy or rich, a withdrawal when poor), had to be told each of these. Idea: put all three in the first-hour page (the guide's Money and shops page already has the bank words) and make the refusals say what to do ("Step next to the corpse").

### Not findings (checked and fine)

- Death and recovery work without staff: the ghost walked about 100 tiles to the healer in under a minute and the gump accepted.
- Magic Arrow and Heal cast and train; shop prices and stock are sensible.
- The Welcome guide opens on `[Welcome` with a first-hour page; its text could carry items 1, 6, 7 and 8 above without any code.
- The "Generic Player" grey character the bot kept seeing was the staff test account, not a game problem.

## Things the bot could not or did not cover

Night and darkness (Night Sight), the bank, the second circle onward (the mage never gained the skill to unlock more), a second character in a party, theft, and the real client's own screens beyond the first one. Each would be a new rule in the tree.
